What Is LRS?

The Liberalised Remittance Scheme, or LRS, is the framework under which eligible resident individuals can remit money outside India for permitted purposes. Common use cases include education fees, living expenses, travel, overseas investments and family maintenance.

The bank or authorised dealer will usually ask for the purpose of remittance, documents and tax information before processing the transfer.

Foreign remittance

What Is TCS on Foreign Remittance?

TCS means Tax Collected at Source. In eligible foreign remittance cases, the authorised dealer may collect tax at the time of processing the remittance.

For many taxpayers, TCS is not a final extra cost. It is generally available as credit while filing the Income Tax Return, subject to the applicable tax provisions and correct reporting in tax records.

Why Purpose Code Matters

The purpose selected during remittance affects documentation, bank checks and TCS treatment. Selecting the wrong purpose can create avoidable reconciliation problems later.

  • Education tuition fees and living expenses.
  • Overseas tour packages and travel-related payments.
  • Foreign investments under permitted routes.
  • Family maintenance or support payments.
  • Medical treatment and related overseas expenses.

How TCS Affects Different Remitters

Students and parents should keep admission letters, fee invoices and bank confirmations ready. Education payments may have different documentation and tax treatment depending on how the payment is funded.

Travellers should check whether their payment is a tour package, forex purchase or card-related spend. Investors should keep records of overseas investment remittances and purpose declarations.

NRIs should not assume that NRI status alone answers every TCS question. The tax treatment depends on the resident remitter, purpose and applicable provision.

How to Claim TCS Credit in ITR

After TCS is collected, taxpayers should check whether the amount appears in their tax records. The credit should be matched with remittance documents and claimed correctly while filing the Income Tax Return.

  • Download and preserve bank remittance advice.
  • Check Form 26AS/AIS/TIS before filing the return.
  • Match PAN, remitter name and TCS amount.
  • Claim eligible credit in the correct ITR schedule.
  • Retain documents in case of future clarification.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Treating TCS as a permanent tax cost instead of checking ITR credit eligibility.
  • Selecting the wrong remittance purpose code.
  • Ignoring exchange-rate impact while planning the payment.
  • Forgetting to reconcile TCS before filing ITR.
  • Not keeping education, travel or investment documents.
  • Relying on old thresholds or rates without checking current bank guidance.

How SBI TT Rates Fit Into Planning

TCS is only one part of the total outflow. The Telegraphic Transfer buying or selling rate used by the bank can materially affect large remittances such as university fees, overseas investments or property-related payments.

Before remitting, compare the applicable exchange rate, bank charges and expected TCS cash-flow impact. TaxBro's SBI TT Rates page helps users track daily rate movement for planning and recordkeeping.

Pre-Remittance Checklist

  • Confirm whether the remittance is covered under LRS.
  • Select the correct purpose code.
  • Verify current TCS treatment with the authorised dealer.
  • Check the latest exchange rate and bank charges.
  • Keep PAN and supporting documents ready.
  • Save remittance receipts and bank confirmations.
  • Reconcile TCS credit while filing ITR.
FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is TCS on foreign remittance an extra tax?

Not always. In many cases, TCS is available as credit against income tax liability while filing the ITR, subject to applicable provisions.

Does TCS apply to every LRS remittance?

No. Applicability depends on the purpose, threshold, category and law applicable at the time of remittance. Confirm the current treatment with the bank before transfer.

Why does the bank ask for a purpose code?

The purpose code tells the bank why money is being sent abroad. It affects documentation, reporting and sometimes tax treatment.

Can students claim TCS credit?

Eligible TCS reflected against the student's or remitter's PAN may generally be considered while filing the relevant ITR, subject to tax records and applicable rules.

Do SBI TT rates affect remittance planning?

Yes. Exchange rates affect the rupee amount needed for a foreign currency payment. For large payments, even small rate movements matter.

What documents should I keep?

Keep bank advice, payment invoices, admission letters for education, travel invoices, investment documents and proof of TCS collection.
